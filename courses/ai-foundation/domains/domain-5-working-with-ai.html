Learning outcomes

By the end of this domain, candidates will be able to:

  • Use AI to produce first drafts and to reshape content for a different audience
  • Review and personalise AI output rather than using it unchanged
  • Produce directed, audience-targeted summaries and recognise the risk of omissions
  • Use AI as a thinking partner while retaining human ownership of the result
  • Determine when AI is suitable and when human expertise or authoritative sources should lead
  • Apply the verification habit to facts, figures, dates, names, quotes and citations
  • Explain why the user, not the AI, remains accountable

What this subject is about

What this domain covers, with teaching notes area by area.

5.1, Drafting & Rewriting

AI produces competent first drafts quickly and can reshape existing content four ways: shortening, re-levelling for a new audience, changing tone, and changing format. The session rule: AI produces the first draft; the person reviews, personalises and owns. Would you sign your name to it? If not, revise it, tone, invented detail and missing context are exactly what slips through unchanged output.

5.2, Summarising

A summary is a directed artefact: audience and purpose shape what stays in. A board summary and a team summary of the same document are different documents. The caution is core: compression silently drops detail, exceptions, caveats, small print. For high-stakes documents (contracts, regulations, safety text, anything you cannot afford to misread) the rule is simple: read the original.

5.3, Ideation, Planning & Research Assistance

Position AI as a thinking partner: it generates options, structures plans and stress-tests ideas. Human ownership of the result is non-negotiable, the plan is yours. Research assistance is a starting point to verify, never an authoritative source; never cite the AI itself as a source, because the AI is not a source, it generates plausible text.

5.4, Appropriate Use

Judge suitability by task and stakes: what does it cost to be wrong? Low-stakes internal drafting, draft freely. Customer-facing material, verify before use. High-stakes judgement (legal, medical, safety, people decisions), human expertise leads. The test is not convenience; it is the stakes of error.

5.5, The Verification Habit

Before acting on or sharing output, verify facts, figures, dates, names, quotes and citations, at the primary source, not by asking another AI. The four-step ritual from the session: list the claims in the output; go to the primary source for each; confirm what matches and flag what does not; record what you checked. The user, not the AI, remains accountable for what is shared. GOV.UK research found 84% of AI-adopting businesses already do at least some human checking, the habit is normal, and it is the syllabus.

5.6, Personal Prompt Library

Awareness level: a personal prompt library is a collection of reusable, tested prompts for recurring tasks, reports, emails, minutes. Save what works, organise it, test it, keep it current. A library is only useful if it is organised, tested and shared.
