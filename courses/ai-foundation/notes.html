5. Variability and embeddings

Sub-area 2.5, variability and embeddings.

Domain 2 in six lines

  • A model is millions of settings, tuned by training, then frozen
  • Inference is simply using it, and it never learns from your chat
  • Text is generated one token at a time, by predicting what comes next
  • Tokens fill a finite context window - what falls off the desk is gone
  • Every model has a knowledge cut-off; newer facts must be supplied
  • Sampling causes variability; embeddings turn meaning into numbers

Glossary of key terms

Artificial intelligence. Machines performing tasks we associate with human intelligence; in practice, learning patterns from data.

Machine learning. The part of AI that improves from data rather than following rules a person wrote.

Deep learning. Machine learning using layered neural networks.

Generative AI. Deep learning that produces new content: text, images, audio or code.

Narrow AI. A system built for one task or a narrow family of tasks. All deployed AI today is narrow.

General AI. A hypothetical system matching a person across any task. It does not exist today.

Token. A chunk of text, roughly part of a word, that a language model processes.

Context window. The amount of text a model can consider at once.

Knowledge cut-off. The date after which a model has no built-in knowledge.

Embedding. A numeric representation of meaning that lets a model compare items by similarity.

Hallucination. Fluent, confident output that is simply wrong.

Prompt. The instruction you give a model.

Few-shot prompting. Including examples in a prompt to show the model the pattern you want.

Retrieval-augmented generation. Supplying a model with retrieved documents so its answer is grounded in real sources.

Agent. A system that plans and takes multiple steps towards a goal using tools.

Shadow AI. Using unapproved AI tools for official work.

Prompt injection. Hidden instructions inside content that trick a model into ignoring its real task.

Bias. A systematic unfairness in a model's output, usually inherited from its data or design.

Human oversight. A person remaining responsible for consequential decisions.

Disclosure. Telling people when AI has been used to produce something that affects them.

Quick revision checklist

  • I can define AI and say what it is not.
  • I can put AI, machine learning, deep learning and generative AI in the right order.
  • I know training from inference, and why inference never learns.
  • I can explain tokens, the context window and the knowledge cut-off.
  • I can name the four working categories of AI task.
  • I can tell a chatbot from an agent.
  • I can write a prompt with a task, context, format and constraints.
  • I know few-shot prompting and when to decompose a task.
  • I verify facts, figures, sums and citations before they leave.
  • I can spot hallucination, staleness and fabrication.
  • I know the protective ladder and what never goes into a public tool.
  • I can defeat a deepfake or a cloned voice out of band.
  • I can explain bias, transparency, explainability and accountability.
  • I know when to disclose AI use.
  • I can apply the six-question decision framework.
  • I know the exam format: 120 questions, 70 per cent, 120 minutes, closed book.
