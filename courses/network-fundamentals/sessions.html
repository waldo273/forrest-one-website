The Twelve Sessions

The course is divided into twelve sessions: a three-session network foundation followed by the nine-session Security+ core.

How to read a session

Each session page lists the areas it covers, the learning outcomes a candidate should be able to demonstrate, and the resources available for that session, including the narrated session video and the section quiz.

Session 1

Network Foundations: Networking Models & IP Addressing

The language of networks: the OSI and TCP/IP models, encapsulation and port addressing, then the IPv4/IPv6 addressing engine — CIDR, subnetting, VLSM — that security controls such as firewalls, ACLs and segmentation rely on.

  • 1A — Network Models
  • 1B — IPv4 Addressing
  • 1C — VLSM
  • 1D — IPv6
 Session 2

Switching, VLANs & Network Architectures

Layer 2 in action: MAC addresses and switch forwarding, VLAN segmentation and 802.1Q trunking, Spanning Tree loop prevention — the mechanisms that carry traffic and the controls that keep segments apart.

  • 2A — Layer 2 Switching
  • 2B — VLANs & Trunking
  • 2C — Spanning Tree
  • 2D — Architectures
 Session 3

Network Services & the Attack Surface

The services that make IP networks usable — DNS, DHCP, NTP and SSH — and the attacks that target each: poisoning, rogue servers, amplification and brute force, with the Kali commands that verify them.

  • 3A — DNS
  • 3B — DHCP
  • 3C — NTP
  • 3D — SSH
 Session 4

Fundamental Security Concepts

The foundational principles of information security: the CIA triad and non-repudiation, the AAA framework, security control categories and functional types, the NIST Cybersecurity Framework, gap analysis, and the roles and responsibilities that make security governance work.

  • 1A — Security Concepts
  • 1B — Security Controls
  • 1C — Roles & Responsibilities
 Session 5

Threats, Attack Types & Threat Actors

Comparing threat actor types and their motivations; explaining threat vectors and attack surfaces; and understanding the vulnerability-threat-risk relationship that underpins every defensive decision.

  • 2A — Threat Actors
  • 2B — Attack Surface
 Session 6

Cryptography & Public Key Infrastructure

Symmetric and asymmetric encryption, hashing and digital signatures; the public key infrastructure of certificates, authorities and revocation; and the practical solutions for data at rest, in transit and in use.

  • 3A — Cryptographic Algorithms
  • 3B — Public Key Infrastructure
  • 3C — Cryptographic Solutions
 Session 7

Identity & Access Management

Authentication factors and password concepts, multifactor and biometric authentication, hard and soft tokens, account policies and authorisation, and single sign-on with federated identity.

  • 4A — Authentication
  • 4B — Account Policies & Authorisation
  • 4C — SSO & Federation
 Session 8

Enterprise Network Security: Firewalls, IDS, IPS, VPNs & Secure Protocols

Architecture and segmentation, the appliance stack — firewalls, IDS/IPS, proxies, WAF and NAC — secure remote access with VPN and ZTNA, and the secure protocol toolkit that carries traffic safely.

  • 8A — Architecture
  • 8B — Security Appliances
  • 8C — Secure Remote Access
  • 8D — Secure Protocols
 Session 9

Endpoint, Application, Cloud & Virtualisation Security

Hardening the hosts, securing the code and defending the shared estate: endpoint protection and management, the SDLC and secure coding, application attacks, and cloud and virtualisation security with shared responsibility.

  • 9A — Endpoint Security
  • 9B — Application Lifecycle
  • 9C — Application Attacks
  • 9D — Cloud & Virtualisation
 Session 10

Vulnerability Management, Resiliency & Asset Protection

The continuous hunt and the safety net: vulnerability identification, assessment, remediation and disclosure — then resiliency, redundancy, backups, and the physical and environmental controls that keep the business running.

  • 10A — Identification
  • 10B — Assessment
  • 10C — Remediation
  • 10D — Reporting & Disclosure
  • 10E — Resiliency
  • 10F — Site Security
 Session 11

Security Operations: Monitoring, IR & Forensics

Alerting and monitoring concepts, the incident response lifecycle, digital forensics and evidence handling, and the indicators of malicious activity across the estate.

  • 11A — Monitoring
  • 11B — Incident Response
  • 11C — Forensics
  • 11D — Indicators
 Session 12

Governance, Risk, Compliance & Exam Consolidation

The layer that turns security into organisational behaviour: policies and standards, risk management, compliance, and the protection of data and privacy.

  • 12A — Governance
  • 12B — Risk Management
  • 12C — Data Protection
