Core concepts

1A - Security concepts

The CIA triad is the foundation: confidentiality (read only by authorised persons), integrity (data stored and transferred as intended), availability (accessible when needed). Each pillar has distinct threats and controls: confidentiality is threatened by interception and data theft, protected by encryption and access controls; integrity by unauthorised tampering, protected by hashing and digital signatures; availability by denial of service and hardware failure, protected by redundancy, backups and failover. Non-repudiation adds proof of origin and action via digital signatures and audit logs. The AAA framework sequences access: identify, authenticate, authorise, account - authentication rests on three factors (something you know, have, or are). The NIST CSF functions - identify, protect, detect, respond, recover - structure a whole programme, and gap analysis scores maturity against it.

1B - Security controls

Controls are classified on two axes. Category: managerial (policies and risk assessments), operational (guard patrols, incident response), technical (firewalls, ACLs, IDS, encryption), physical (locks, fences, cameras, mantraps). Functional type: preventive (blocks before - firewalls, access controls), detective (finds during - IDS, log monitoring), corrective (fixes after - patching, restore), directive (guides behaviour - acceptable use policies), deterrent (discourages - warning banners, visible cameras), compensating (substitutes when a primary control is not feasible - enhanced logging in place of encryption).

1C - Roles & principles

CISO/CSO owns strategy; ISSO manages day-to-day; data owner is accountable; custodian implements; analyst monitors and responds; auditor verifies independently. Principles: defence in depth (layered controls so no single point of failure), least privilege (only the access needed), separation of duties (no one person controls a critical process alone), zero trust (never trust, always verify; microsegmentation and continuous verification; a design principle, not a product).

Memory aids

CIA - Read, Unchanged, Accessible: confidentiality is the read side, integrity the change side, availability the access side.AAA - I A A A: the user is identified, then authenticated, then authorised, then accounted.Controls on two axes: category asks who or what (managerial, operational, technical, physical); type asks when and how it acts (preventive to compensating).Non-repudiation means deniable, no: signatures and logs make denial fail.Zero trust - never trust, always verify: a design principle, not a product.

Practical cautions

Where marks are lost

  • Do not confuse integrity (unchanged) with correctness (true).
  • Do not confuse preventive (blocks) with deterrent (discourages).
  • Do not confuse the data owner (accountable) with the custodian (implementer).
  • Do not treat a vulnerability scan as a gap analysis - they measure different things.
  • Do not expect zero trust to be a product - it is a design principle.

Exam tips for Session 04

Security+ alignment

  • Know the two-axis control model cold - category and functional type, with one concrete example for each.
  • Non-repudiation is a favourite exam term - link it to signatures and logs, not logging alone.
  • Learn the classic paired distinctions: preventive versus deterrent, integrity versus correctness, owner versus custodian, scan versus gap analysis.
  • Zero trust is described as a design principle - reject any option that sells it as a product.
  • The exam is vendor-neutral - no product-specific questions.
  • Learn the exam facts: 90 questions, 90 minutes, pass mark 750.

Self-check

Answer these from memory before you take the quiz. If you cannot answer one, revisit the matching concept above.

  1. Define the three pillars of the CIA triad and give one control for each.
  2. What does non-repudiation provide, and how?
  3. Name the four steps of the AAA framework and the three authentication factors.
  4. Give an example control for each of the four categories.
  5. Which functional type operates during an attack? Which after?
  6. What are the five NIST CSF functions?
  7. Explain the difference between a vulnerability scan and a gap analysis.
  8. Who is accountable for a data set, and who implements the controls that protect it?
  9. What does microsegmentation do in a zero-trust model?
