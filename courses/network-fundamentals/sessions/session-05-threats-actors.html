Core concepts

2A - Threat actors

Risk = threat × vulnerability: a threat that exploits a vulnerability creates risk. Actors are compared by behaviour (targeted or opportunistic), source (internal or external), sophistication, resources and funding, and motivation with intent. Types: lone hackers, script kiddies, hacker teams and hacktivists, nation-state actors and APTs, organised crime, competitors, and insiders (malicious and unintentional). Motivations: financial, political or ideological, strategic, and personal.

2B - Attack surface & vectors

The attack surface spans hardware, software, networks, processes and human factors, and grows as cloud, mobile and IoT devices are added. Vectors: direct access (USB, console), remote access (VPN, RDP, SSH), wireless and cloud, open ports, lure-based (Trojan, malicious documents, phishing, spear phishing, whaling, smishing, vishing, watering holes), vulnerable software (unpatched systems, misconfigurations, zero-days, unsupported systems, default credentials), and the supply chain (compromised updates, vendor credentials). The attack chain: recon, vector, exploit, act.

Practical cautions

Where marks are lost

  • Do not call all hackers criminals; white hats are ethical.
  • Do not forget unintentional insiders; most insider incidents are accidents.
  • Do not use 'APT' for every advanced attack; persistence and strategy are the point.
  • Do not ignore the human vectors; phishing is the most common entry point.
  • Do not assume patching fixes everything; unsupported systems and zero-days have no patch.
  • Do not treat vulnerability and risk as the same thing; risk needs a threat behind the weakness.

Exam tips for Session 05

Security+ alignment

  • Scenario questions reward classification; label the actor, the vector and the motivation.
  • APT = persistence and strategy, not just sophistication.
  • Insider threat includes accidents, not just malice.
  • Supply chain is an increasingly tested vector; know the NotPetya update example.
  • Know the difference between a vulnerability and a risk; the exam tests the distinction directly.
  • Lure-based vectors cover phishing in all its forms, from broad to spear phishing and whaling.

Key terms

TermDefinition
VulnerabilityA weakness in a system, process or design
ThreatA person, tool or method that could exploit a vulnerability
RiskThe likelihood and impact of a threat exploiting a vulnerability
Threat actorA person or group that poses a threat
APTAdvanced persistent threat, long-term and strategic, state-backed or highly resourced
Script kiddieUnskilled attacker using pre-written tools on easy targets
HacktivistHacker promoting a political or social cause
Spear phishingTargeted, personalised phishing aimed at a named individual
Zero dayA vulnerability unknown to the vendor with no available patch
False flagTechniques that mislead attribution to another entity

Self-check

Answer these from memory before you take the quiz. If you cannot answer one, revisit the matching concept above.

  1. Define vulnerability, threat and risk in one sentence each.
  2. Name the five attributes used to compare threat actors.
  3. List five threat actor types and one motivation for each.
  4. What makes an APT different from a single advanced attack?
  5. Name six threat vectors and give one example of each.
  6. What are the four steps of the attack chain?
  7. Contrast an unpatched-software vector with a supply chain vector, using one real example for each.
  8. Explain why most insider incidents are not malicious, and name two defences.
