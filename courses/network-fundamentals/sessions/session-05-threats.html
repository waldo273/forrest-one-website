Threats, Attack Types & Threat Actors

Comparing threat actor types and their motivations; explaining threat vectors and attack surfaces; and understanding the vulnerability-threat-risk relationship that underpins every defensive decision.

Learning outcomes

By the end of this session, candidates will be able to:

  • Define vulnerability, threat and risk, and explain how they combine.
  • Describe the attributes of threat actors: behaviour, source, sophistication, resources and funding.
  • Compare threat actor types: lone hackers, unskilled attackers, hacker teams and hacktivists, nation-state actors and APTs, organised crime, competitors, and internal actors.
  • Explain threat actor motivations: financial, political, strategic and personal.
  • Define the attack surface and the factors that expand it.
  • Explain threat vectors: direct access, remote access, wireless and cloud, lure-based, vulnerable software, and supply chain.
  • Describe the attack chain: reconnaissance, vector, exploit, act.

What this session is about

What this session covers, with teaching notes area by area.

2A — Threat actors

Risk = threat × vulnerability: a threat that exploits a vulnerability creates risk. Actors are compared by behaviour (targeted or opportunistic), source (internal or external), sophistication, resources and funding, and motivation with intent. Types: lone hackers, script kiddies, hacker teams and hacktivists, nation-state actors and APTs, organised crime, competitors, and insiders (malicious and unintentional). Motivations: financial, political or ideological, strategic, and personal.

2B — Attack surface & vectors

The attack surface spans hardware, software, networks, processes and human factors, and grows as cloud, mobile and IoT devices are added. Vectors: direct access (USB, console), remote access (VPN, RDP, SSH), wireless and cloud, open ports, lure-based (Trojan, malicious documents, phishing, spear phishing, whaling, smishing, vishing, watering holes), vulnerable software (unpatched systems, misconfigurations, zero-days, unsupported systems, default credentials), and the supply chain (compromised updates, vendor credentials). The attack chain: recon, vector, exploit, act.
