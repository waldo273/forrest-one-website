Identity & Access Management

Authentication factors and password concepts, multifactor and biometric authentication, hard and soft tokens, account policies and authorisation, and single sign-on with federated identity.

4A4B4C

Learning outcomes

By the end of this session, candidates will be able to:

  • Name the authentication factors: something you know, have, are/do, and somewhere you are.
  • Describe password concepts: length and complexity, aging, reuse, NIST guidance, and password hints as a vector.
  • Explain password managers: vault, generation, secure filling, form factors.
  • Define multifactor authentication and identify the trap of combining two factors of the same type.
  • Describe biometric authentication, including FAR and FRR.
  • Compare hard tokens (smart card, OTP, FIDO/U2F, TOTP) and soft tokens (SMS, push, authenticator apps), and explain passwordless authentication.
  • Describe account types and account policies: provisioning and the user lifecycle, lockout, time-of-day restrictions, auditing, and identity governance.
  • Explain authorisation models: permissions, least privilege, RBAC and ABAC, and privileged access management with just-in-time access.
  • Explain single sign-on and its concentration risk, and how directory services underpin it.
  • Explain federated identity: identity provider, service provider, and the protocols SAML, OAuth 2.0, OpenID Connect, LDAP and Kerberos.

What this session is about

What this session covers, with teaching notes area by area.

4A — Authentication

Factors: know, have, are/do, somewhere. MFA requires different categories — password+PIN is one factor. Passwords: length over complexity, no hints, managers for uniqueness. Biometrics: FAR and FRR trade off. Hard tokens (smart card, OTP, FIDO) resist phishing; soft tokens (SMS, push, apps) are convenient but some are interceptable. Passwordless uses authenticator-held keys.

4B — Account policies & authorisation

Account types: user, privileged, service, shared, guest. Provisioning manages the user lifecycle — onboard, modify on role change, offboard promptly. Policies: lockout, time-of-day, auditing. Authorisation: permissions, least privilege, RBAC (roles), ABAC (attributes). Privileged accounts need PAM: just-in-time access, password vaulting and session recording. Identity governance reviews access, enforces policy and audits.

4C — SSO & federation

SSO: one authentication, many apps — removes password fatigue but concentrates risk, mitigated by MFA and monitoring. Directory services (Active Directory, LDAP) are the source of truth for identities. Federation: IdP authenticates and issues a token, SP trusts the assertion. Protocols: SAML (assertions), OAuth 2.0 (authorisation), OIDC (identity on OAuth), LDAP (directory), Kerberos (tickets).
