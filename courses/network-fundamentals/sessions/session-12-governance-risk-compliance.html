Governance, Risk, Compliance & Exam Consolidation

The layer that turns security into organisational behaviour: policies and standards, risk management, compliance, and the protection of data and privacy.

Learning outcomes

By the end of this session, candidates will be able to:

  • Explain the governance stack: policy, standard, procedure, guideline.
  • Describe common security policies and their audiences.
  • Explain regulatory and industry compliance, and which applies to an organisation.
  • Describe the risk management process: identify, assess, respond, monitor.
  • Compare qualitative and quantitative risk assessment, including ALE, SLE and ARO.
  • Explain risk responses: avoid, transfer, mitigate, accept.
  • Describe vendor and supply chain risk management.
  • Explain data classification levels and the data lifecycle.
  • Describe data roles: owner, steward, custodian.
  • Explain privacy law: GDPR/UK GDPR principles, rights, and the breach notification duty.
  • Explain data sovereignty and its implications for cloud and jurisdiction.

What this session is about

What this session covers, with teaching notes area by area.

12A — Governance

Policy states intent; standards set mandatory requirements; procedures give steps; guidelines advise. Documents form a hierarchy and run a lifecycle of develop, approve, distribute, review. Compliance: regulatory (law) vs industry (sector practice). Which apply depends on industry, geography and data types. Compliance sets a baseline — security must go beyond it for emerging threats.

12B — Risk management

Process: identify, assess, respond, monitor. Identification uses asset inventories, threat modelling and vulnerability assessments. Qualitative = ratings; quantitative = money (SLE = AV × EF; ALE = SLE × ARO). Responses: avoid, transfer, mitigate, accept — acceptance is documented, management-level. The risk register records risks, owners, ratings and treatment status, and is reviewed regularly. Vendor risk: assess before contract, monitor during, exit cleanly.

12C — Data protection

Classification: public, internal, confidential, restricted. States: at rest, in transit, in use. Roles: owner (accountable), steward (governance), custodian (implementation). Lifecycle with retention and secure destruction. GDPR/UK GDPR: principles, consent, rights, 72-hour breach duty. Sovereignty: jurisdiction follows the data and the data subject's location.
