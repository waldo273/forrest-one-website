Core concepts

12A - Governance

Policy states intent; standards set mandatory requirements; procedures give steps; guidelines advise. Documents form a hierarchy and run a lifecycle of develop, approve, distribute, review. Compliance: regulatory (law) vs industry (sector practice). Which apply depends on industry, geography and data types. Compliance sets a baseline - security must go beyond it for emerging threats.

12B - Risk management

Process: identify, assess, respond, monitor. Identification uses asset inventories, threat modelling and vulnerability assessments. Qualitative = ratings; quantitative = money (SLE = AV × EF; ALE = SLE × ARO). Responses: avoid, transfer, mitigate, accept - acceptance is documented, management-level. The risk register records risks, owners, ratings and treatment status, and is reviewed regularly. Vendor risk: assess before contract, monitor during, exit cleanly.

12C - Data protection

Classification: public, internal, confidential, restricted. States: at rest, in transit, in use. Roles: owner (accountable), steward (governance), custodian (implementation). Lifecycle with retention and secure destruction. GDPR/UK GDPR: principles, consent, rights, 72-hour breach duty. Sovereignty: jurisdiction follows the data and the data subject's location.

Practical cautions

Where marks are lost

  • Do not confuse policy (intent) with standard (requirements).
  • Do not treat guidelines as mandatory.
  • Do not accept risk without management sign-off and documentation.
  • Do not keep data beyond retention - destroy it securely.
  • Do not ignore which jurisdiction your cloud data lives in.
  • Do not swap the data owner (accountable) with the custodian (implementer).
  • Do not treat compliance as the whole of security - go beyond the baseline.

Exam tips for Session 12

Security+ alignment

  • The exam pairs: policy/standard, qualitative/quantitative, avoid/accept, owner/custodian.
  • Risk response questions are scenario-based - match the response to the situation.
  • Expect quantitative items that supply AV, EF and ARO and ask for SLE or ALE.
  • GDPR items: principles, consent, rights, 72-hour notification, penalties.
  • Data roles: owner accountable, custodian implements - never reversed.
  • Vendor items test the lifecycle: assess before contract, monitor, exit cleanly.

Self-check

Answer these from memory before you take the quiz. If you cannot answer one, revisit the matching concept above.

  1. Define policy, standard, procedure and guideline.
  2. What is the difference between qualitative and quantitative risk assessment, and how is ALE calculated?
  3. Name the four risk responses and one example of each.
  4. What is the difference between the data owner and the data custodian?
  5. Name four GDPR principles, two data rights, and the breach notification window.
  6. What does data sovereignty mean for cloud region choice?
  7. Name three methods used to identify risks during the risk process.
